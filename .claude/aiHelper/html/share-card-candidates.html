Share card + icon candidates — TICKET-10

Twenty-five Open Graph cards, each drawn at its true 1200×630 and scaled to half, plus twelve icons at the sizes that actually ship. Every one of them says word game through its shape — a guess board, a crossword, a rack of letter tiles — before any text is read. Palette is dark grey, green, teal and yellow, nothing else. Reply with one card number and one icon number.

The one technical constraint. The card is generated by Next's ImageResponse, which ships a single font that covers six Greek letters — Λ Ω λ μ π ω — and nothing else: no accents, no final ς. Cards built from those six, from Latin letters, or from digits are free. Cards with real Greek words are marked needs a font and cost one font file committed to the repo (~350 KB). Everything else about them is identical, so the mark is about cost, not quality.

Whatever you pick, I rebuild it in the generator — these are drawn in the same flexbox subset the generator understands, so what you see is what deploys.

Icons — 180 px home-screen tile, 64, 32 px tab, 16 px dense tab

1. Scoring tile, yellow

A letter with its score in the corner — word game in one square. The corner digit survives to 32 px and blurs out at 16, which is fine.

2. Scoring tile, teal

Same tile, cooler. Holds up better against a yellow-heavy browser theme and against the yellow card.

3. Mini guess board (2×2)

Four cells: two green, one yellow, one grey. No letter at all, so no font decision ever — and it is still unmistakably a daily word game.

4. Guess row (3 cells)

One row instead of a square: green, yellow, grey. The most legible of the letterless marks at 16 px because the shapes are big.

5. Crossword cross

Five cells in a plus, the middle one yellow. A hundred years of crossword shorthand compressed into a favicon.

6. Λ built from blocks

The mosaic mark from cards 20 and 21, shrunk. At 16 px it stops being a letter and becomes a texture — check it in the strip below before choosing it.

7. Two crossing tiles

A vertical and a horizontal tile overlapping — a crossword intersection reduced to two rectangles. Abstract, and the most logo-like of the twelve.

8. Tile stack

Three tiles behind one another, top one lit. Suggests a pile of daily puzzles waiting; softest edge of the set.

9. Green tile, white Λ

The plain one, in the strongest of the four colours. Nothing clever; the safest pick and the least distinguishable from any other app.

10. Dark tile, yellow Λ

Inverted. Vanishes into dark browser chrome, which is now most people's setting — worth seeing at 16 px before deciding.

11. Quarter board

Four squares, one per colour family, with a gap grid — a board cropped to its corner. Letterless, and it matches the block cards.

12. Tile with a base rule

A letter tile sitting on a coloured rack line. Reads as a physical tile rather than a coloured square, even at small sizes.
